About checking

Every check spawns helper processes and opens the device, so the interval is a real cost. Pressing the key always reads immediately.

Adapt spends checks where they matter: 15 sec while charging, 30 sec when low, backing off to 10 min while the level holds and 2 min while the device is off. It never goes slower than your interval when charging or low, and any change snaps it back.

About names and offline keys

Stream Deck's own Title field is switched off for this action, so Nickname is the only place a key is named. Deck title writes Stream Deck's title over the image as well, in its font rather than the key's — set it to Nickname and it follows the name above, falling back to the device's own when that's empty.

Last level when off keeps showing what a disconnected device last reported, faded and marked with a crossed-out circle, with its age on the name line. Turn it off for a dash instead.

Time left puts an estimate on the name line instead of the device name — there's room for one of them.

It reads measuring… until the device has actually dropped 3% over at least ten minutes: a rate needs a real fall to measure, and a device that reports in 10% steps can take a while to produce one. It goes back to measuring after a charge, since a discharge rate can't be read across one.

Which devices report a level
Headsets
HyperX, SteelSeries, Corsair, Logitech, Razer, Turtle Beach, Roccat, Astro — around 100 models. Needs HeadsetControl installed and on your PATH; without it, no headset reports a level.
Mice & keyboards
Logitech, over its own receivers or paired directly. Asus ROG. Razer new
Controllers
PlayStation: DualSense, DualSense Edge, DualShock 4 new — over USB or Bluetooth. Xbox new — Bluetooth only, and in four steps rather than a percentage.
Bluetooth LE
Phones, watches, styluses, and anything else that publishes a battery service — Windows reads it, so no brand list applies.
Everything else
Still listed, without a level. Cable-powered devices show a plug instead.

Not covered: SteelSeries, Corsair and Roccat mice and keyboards — each model speaks its own protocol. Xbox pads through the wireless dongle or a cable. Nintendo pads. AirPods, which report over Apple's own protocol rather than Bluetooth's.

Entries marked new were written from published protocol documentation but haven't been tried on the hardware yet.

How devices are found

Nothing is hard-coded per model: the plugin enumerates what this machine can see, so the list is whatever is plugged in or paired right now. A device released tomorrow works if it speaks a protocol above.

Bluetooth LE devices report a level and nothing else, so for those the bolt appears once the level is seen rising. Wireless levels are coarse — many report in steps of 10% — so expect the number to jump.
