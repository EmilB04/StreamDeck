Below 10 seconds there would be nothing new to show: readings are cached for 10 seconds so the key and this panel don't scan in lockstep. Every check spawns a couple of helper processes and opens the device, so adaptive spends them where they matter: every 15 sec while charging and every 30 sec once the level is low (never slower than the interval above), backing off to as much as 10 min while the reading holds steady, and to 2 min while the device is off. Any change snaps it back to your interval. A device that is switched off or out of range keeps showing the level it last reported — faded, and marked with a crossed-out circle. Layout
To get your own title drawn in the key's style, hide Stream Deck's title with the "T" toggle next to the Title field. Stream Deck paints its title on top of the plugin's image and won't let a plugin clear a title you typed, so while it is visible the plugin leaves the name line to the device name instead of printing the same text twice. Stream Deck ignores plugin titles on a key where you typed your own title — clear the Title field above to let this take effect.

0% 50% 10% 90% off 50% In percent. Above the medium threshold the meter uses the high colour. Set the warning to 0 to disable it; above 0, the key flashes Stream Deck's warning icon on every check while a live reading is under it (a last known level never triggers it).
Colours
While charging the key shows a bolt in the top left and breathes slowly in the charging colour. Devices whose battery Windows reports as a bare percentage — phones and other Bluetooth LE devices — can't say whether they're charging, so for those the bolt appears once the level is seen rising.
About

Shows the battery level of a wireless peripheral on a key. Nothing is hard-coded per model: the plugin enumerates what this machine can actually see, so the device list is whatever is plugged in or paired right now.

Levels are read through whichever of these fits the device: HeadsetControl for gaming headsets (a separate tool, and it must be on your PATH), HID++ for Logitech receivers and devices, raw USB/HID reports for Asus devices, and the Windows Bluetooth battery property for anything paired over Bluetooth LE.

A device with no readable battery is still listed, marked "no battery data", so you can tell "not supported" apart from "not detected". Battery levels from wireless devices are coarse by nature — many report in steps of 10% or in four levels, so expect the number to move in jumps.
